Every Massachusetts school gets an annual **accountability determination** from DESE. Part of it is a **percentile from 1 to 99** that ranks the school against others statewide — 99 is near the top, 1 is near the bottom. In its most recent determination, Lynn English sits at a **percentile of 1**, with the classification *"Requiring assistance or intervention"* and a federal **Comprehensive Support and Improvement (CSI)** designation.

That is a low number, and it should be read plainly. But it helps to know what the number is made of. The percentile is a **single summary** that rolls up roughly a dozen separate indicators — MCAS achievement and growth, graduation and dropout rates, English-learner progress, and a few others — each scored, weighted, and combined into one figure. The determination also looks separately at the school's *lowest-performing* group of students, so a school can clear its all-students bar and still be classified on the strength of that group.

In other words, "percentile 1" is the headline, not the story. The same data shows *which* indicators pulled the score down and which held up, which is where any real understanding of the school has to start.
