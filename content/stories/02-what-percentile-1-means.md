Every Massachusetts school gets a yearly report card from the state (DESE). Part of it is a **percentile from 1 to 99**. 99 is near the top of the state. 1 is near the bottom. In its most recent report, Lynn English sits at a **percentile of 1**, with the label *"Requiring assistance or intervention"* and a federal **Comprehensive Support and Improvement (CSI)** flag.

That is a low number, and it should be read plainly. It also helps to know what it is made of. The percentile is **one summary** of about a dozen separate measures — MCAS scores and growth, graduation and dropout, English-learner progress, and a few others. The state also looks at the school's *lowest-performing* group of students. A school can do okay overall and still get a low rating because of that group.

In other words, "percentile 1" is the headline, not the whole story. The same data shows *which* pieces pulled the score down and which held up. That is the useful place to start.
